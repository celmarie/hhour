# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Independent venue owners — the person who runs a single bar, café or restaurant and does it hands-on. They are not very technical, and they most often reach for this app **in the middle of a busy service**, standing at the counter with a customer in front of them. Their moment-to-moment job is to **redeem a customer's voucher fast**, without fumbling. Away from the rush, the same person also creates and edits their happy-hour deals and checks how they're doing and what they've earned. One user wearing every hat — owner, marketer, and cashier — so the app has to make each of those feel easy for someone who isn't a "dashboard person."

## Product Purpose

The merchant side of Appie Hour. It lets an independent venue put itself in front of nearby diners — publishing happy-hour deals and prepaid vouchers — then **redeem those vouchers at the counter** and see, in plain terms, how it's going and what it has earned. Success is an owner who can turn a quiet afternoon into covers and settle a redemption in one confident tap, with no POS, no training, and no marketing team.

## Positioning

The one place a local venue turns quiet hours into customers — publish a deal, then redeem it at the till — without a point-of-sale system or a marketing department.

## Brand Personality

Friendly and approachable. It speaks like a helpful colleague, not a control panel: warm, plain-language, and guiding, so a non-technical owner never feels out of their depth. It still has to be trusted with real money, so the warmth sits on top of genuine clarity and steadiness — reassuring, not corporate. It carries the Appie Hour identity (the red pin, the energy of the consumer app) but dials it into a calmer register suited to a working tool.

## Anti-references

A generic template admin panel — the off-the-shelf Bootstrap-style dashboard with no identity of its own. This app should never feel like a stock theme someone dropped the logo into.

## Design Principles

Counter-first. The redeem action is the hero of the experience: big, unmistakable, fast, and usable one-handed during a rush. Everything else yields space to it.

Plain language over jargon. Say "money you can cash out," not "available balance." Speak the owner's words, not the system's.

Reassure through clarity. Because it handles real money and a venue's reputation, every number is legible, every state is obvious, and nothing ever feels risky or ambiguous.

Branded, not templated. It is unmistakably Appie Hour — the red, the pin, the warmth — and never reads as a generic admin theme.

Calm density. Surface what matters at a glance; let the detail live one tap deeper. Fewer things, clearer.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Shaped by where it's used: at the counter, one-handed, often in poor lighting, on a small phone, by someone who isn't technical. That means generous tap targets, high text-and-number contrast, obvious focus and success states, and a redeem flow that's hard to get wrong. Recommended target: WCAG 2.1 AA (to be confirmed). Respect reduced-motion preferences — the consumer app already learned that heavy animation destabilizes the iOS webview.
